    How it works
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Why split at all?
    
        
    In a RAG pipeline you embed pieces of your documents and retrieve the few that match a question. A chunk has to be small enough to embed well and fit in the prompt, and large enough to still make sense on its own. The splitter decides where the cuts go.
    
        
    chunk_size is the ceiling on chunk length. chunk_overlap repeats the tail of one chunk at the head of the next, so a sentence that straddles a cut is still whole in one of them.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Character splitter
    
        
      
          
    1. Split the whole text on one separator (default "\n\n"). The separator is thrown away.
      2. 
          
    2. Merge: add pieces to a buffer one at a time. When the next piece would push the buffer past chunk_size, join the buffer with the separator and emit it as a chunk.
      3. 
          
    3. Overlap: drop pieces from the front of the buffer until what remains is at most chunk_overlap. The remainder starts the next chunk.
      4. 
        
    
        
    It never cuts inside a piece. A 2,000-character paragraph stays a 2,000-character chunk.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Recursive character splitter
    
        
      
          
    1. Take the first separator on the ladder that appears in the text (default ladder: "\n\n", "\n", " ", "").
      2. 
          
    2. Split on it, keeping the separator attached to the piece that follows.
      3. 
          
    3. Pieces smaller than chunk_size go through the same merge step as above.
      4. 
          
    4. A piece that is still too big is split again with the rest of the ladder. That is the recursion.
      5. 
        
    
        
    With "" at the bottom of the ladder, every chunk is guaranteed to fit, and cuts land on the most meaningful boundary available.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Things to try
    
        
      
          
    • Load No blank lines with the character splitter, then switch to recursive.
      • 
          
    • Set chunk_overlap to 0, then raise it, and watch the striped regions grow.
      • 
          
    • Remove "" and " " from the ladder and shrink chunk_size.
      • 
          
    • Add ". " above " " so chunks prefer to end on sentences.
      • 
          
    • Press Play in Step through and watch the buffer fill, flush and keep its tail.
